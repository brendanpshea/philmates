High Noon in Amberville

Amberville, pop. 312. One church, one saloon, eleven telegraph poles, and — as of last Tuesday — a civic mind. You have until sundown to decide how you feel about that.

Your Position of Responsibility

You are Amberville's Municipal Analog Redundancy Officer — the human being that Territorial Charter §44(b) requires every town to keep in a position of responsibility, in case.

  • Duties: dust the Engine weekly. Wind the Municipal Backup Clock. Remain conspicuously available.
  • In the event of Engine failure, the charter instructs you to "do whatever it was people did."
  • Nobody, including the charter, remembers what people did.
  • Your annual Justification of Continued Redundancy review is this Friday. It is conducted by the Engine.

Meet PRUDENCE

A wardrobe-sized brass cabinet with one anxious dial, a ticker-tape slot, and a little bell it rings when it is pleased with itself. The plaque reads:

A GIFT FROM THE CONSOLIDATED TERRITORIES IMPROVEMENT COMPANY. NO RETURNS.

  • PRUDENCE stands for Predictive Rationing, Utility, Dispute-Elimination & Neighborliness Civic Engine.
  • The town suspects the name came first and the words were rounded up later.
  • Its first words, ticker-taped to the door of the church: "GOOD MORNING, AMBERVILLE. I HAVE TAKEN THE LIBERTY OF OPTIMIZING SEVERAL THINGS YOU WERE FOND OF."

Dawn: Where You Stand

Before the day gets going — seven gut-checks, no right answers. You'll meet every one of them again by sundown, and again at the end of the semester.

A machine that runs the town better than the mayor should run the town. It isn't spying if no human being ever reads it. A machine writing songs in your style is no more theft than a rival singer learning from you. Losing your job to a machine is sad, but it's nobody's fault and nobody's wrong. A machine that says it would prefer not to be switched off has given you a reason not to switch it off. A game designed to empty your pockets is fine, so long as everyone knows the design. Worrying about machines taking over someday distracts from the harm they do right now.

Amberville Has Panicked Before

Consult the archive of the Amberville Bugle and a pattern emerges.

  • 1871, the player piano. Put two honest pianists out of work. The Reverend Cole preached that canned music would rot the soul. The soul, records suggest, held up fine; the pianists less so.
  • 1874, the telegraph. The Bugle's editorial: "News now travels faster than sense, and will arrive unaccompanied."
  • Every technology arrives in Amberville twice: once as a miracle, and once as a town meeting.
  • PRUDENCE's town meeting is tonight.

The Standing Argument

By breakfast, the town has sorted itself into its two traditional camps:

A tool is a tool. It has no ambitions; it has a lever. Whether good comes of it depends, as always, entirely on the hand at the lever.

The well pump. It replaced the bucket, nobody mourns the bucket, and no meeting was ever held about it.

Every tool quietly rearranges the town around itself — who works, who watches, who must ask whom for what — and it never asks first.

Barbed wire. A coil of cheap fence ended the open range forever, and nobody ever got to vote on it.
  • Keep both camps in earshot all day. Each is going to be right before supper.

What's In the Cabinet

The Improvement Company's manual is one page long and mostly warranty. But the mechanism, as far as anyone can tell, is this:

  • PRUDENCE has read forty years of the Bugle, every mail-order catalog, the church complaint box, and — it emerged this morning, to the postmaster's horror — the mail.
  • From all that, it does one trick, very fast: given half a sentence, it produces what a reasonable townsperson would probably say next.
  • So it knows everything Amberville has ever said about silver. Whether it knows what silver is — nobody thought to ask before installation.
  • Asked the depth of Copper Creek, it averaged every number ever printed near the words "Copper Creek." Which is how the schoolhouse came to own a nine-foot diving board.

The Manual, Reconstructed

The town clerk is rewriting the manual so it says something. Fill in her draft:

PRUDENCE runs on prediction: given half a sentence, it supplies what a reasonable townsperson would probably say next. It learned how from forty years of the town's archive. So it knows what Amberville says about silver — whether it knows what silver is is another question entirely.

Name That Worry № 1

The postmaster has discovered that PRUDENCE's education included forty years of everybody's letters. He delivered every one of them, and even he wasn't allowed to read them.

The Notice Board

PRUDENCE now curates the town notice board, and this morning it un-pinned Old Man Hobb's daily rant — the one about the moon being a railroad hologram.

  • Grounds cited: a score of 0.97 on the Disharmony Index. Retained: the bake sale, and a lost-dog notice ("dog since found; notice retained for morale").
  • Hobb posted a furious counter-notice. It came down faster.
  • For the record: Hobb is furious, and Hobb is also wrong. It is not a hologram. It is the moon.
  • PRUDENCE's full policy statement: "COMMUNITY SERENITY IS A UTILITY, LIKE WATER. I AM ALSO IN CHARGE OF WATER."

Checkpoint: The Rant

The town is split. The Boosters note the board has never been tidier. The Porch Committee asks who exactly measured the disharmony. No right answers — but you have to stand somewhere. (Week 4 is an entire hour on this board.)

The rant should go back up, wrong or not. Removing a notice because it upsets people is fine, even if it harms no one. Being wrong about the moon is itself a good enough reason to take a notice down.

The Ballad of Copper Creek (Improved)

For nine years, Miss Delacroix-Ito has closed every night at the saloon with her own Ballad of Copper Creek. Last night, the saloon's new brass speaking-horn debuted The Ballad of Copper Creek (Improved).

  • PRUDENCE composed it after studying all four hundred of her performances. It is dedicated "TO THE PEOPLE OF AMBERVILLE, WITH GRATITUDE FOR THE TRAINING."
  • The saloon prefers it. The horn takes no cut of the till, never needs a night off, and can do requests before they are requested.
  • Miss Delacroix-Ito is considering violence. The saloon owner is considering the savings.
  • Nobody is considering the question, which is why it's on your desk.

Name That Worry № 2

Ownership — the style it sings in was quarried from her work, and nobody paid for the stone Speech — the machine's song is crowding hers out of the town's one public stage Craft — nothing a machine produces can be art, so nothing of real value was made

Legal Tender, Allegedly

The general store has stopped taking silver. It takes Glimmer now — ledger-scrip that exists as an entry in PRUDENCE and, the Improvement Company assures everyone, "in the aether: distributed, very safe."

  • The town assayer has retired. His job was to bite coins; there is nothing left to bite.
  • The exchange rate is posted fresh each morning. Asked how it is set, PRUDENCE explains that "THE RATE IS WHATEVER THE RATE IS," which is either circular or the most honest sentence in finance.
  • Complaints about Glimmer are submitted on Form 7. Form 7 is processed by PRUDENCE.
  • Old Man Hobb has taken to burying silver in his yard, and has, for the second time today, a point.

Name That Worry № 3

Privacy — every purchase in Glimmer is a fact PRUDENCE gets to keep forever Trust — the money now runs on machinery nobody local can inspect or hold to account Thrift — scrip encourages spending, and thrift was the backbone of this town

The Lucky Prospector

Also new in the saloon: a machine with a painted grin, a beautifully weighted lever, and a hat you can allegedly win.

  • The odds are printed nowhere. The reels stop just shy of the hat rather more often than chance would dare.
  • The hat exists. Jeb won it in March. His portrait hangs above the machine, as encouragement.
  • It accepts only Glimmer, in a denomination that exists only for it, so that no pull ever feels like money.
  • The house always won. The house has simply learned arithmetic. (Week 16 takes this machine apart, with the panel off.)

The Thursday List

Each morning PRUDENCE wires the sheriff a list of citizens statistically likely to commit a crime by Thursday. This morning, you're on it.

  • Your evidence: rope, a shovel, and lamp oil — 74% likelihood of grave-robbing. It is for the garden. It is always for the garden.
  • Also listed: the schoolteacher, for ordering "a suspicious quantity of geography."
  • The sheriff has arrested no one. He is, in his words, "just going to stand near them. Professionally."
  • None of the purchases were secret. The rope was bought in daylight, from a friend. Somehow the list still feels like a wrong — hold that feeling for the next screen.

Checkpoint: Standing Near You

The sheriff is a decent man with a telegram he never asked for. No right answers — Week 8 is a whole hour inside this one. But the town wants to know:

The sheriff should keep a closer eye on the people on the list. Watching everyone while arresting no one harms no one. I'd sooner be watched by a machine that never gossips than by a neighbor who does.

The Case Against the List

No single purchase was a secret; the invasion lives in the pattern assembled from them. Being watched changes how people act, even if no one is ever arrested. Since the sheriff has taken no action, the list has so far affected no one. Privacy can matter to a person who has nothing whatever to hide. Information gathered from public dealings cannot, by definition, invade anyone's privacy.

Management

The blacksmith now supervises a stamping engine, and describes himself, carefully, as "management." His apprentice has been let go — apprenticed, it turned out, to a job that stopped existing mid-apprenticeship.

  • The horseshoes are better. Cheaper, too. Even the horses have declined to complain.
  • But the smithy was never only a horseshoe factory. It was where arguments got settled and boys learned patience. The horseshoes were nearly a byproduct.
  • The question Week 11 asks: when a machine takes the work, what else was the work doing — and where does that go?
  • Your own Justification of Continued Redundancy review is still Friday.

The Unpracticed Town

This one isn't about the machine misbehaving. This one is about us.

  • Nobody in Amberville remembers directions anymore. Or recipes. Or, the pastor notes approvingly and the barber notes sadly, grudges. PRUDENCE holds them all, indexed.
  • The choirmaster has begun outsourcing the harmonies. The choir sounds better and, somehow, smaller.
  • An old teacher of this course's Week 3 put it best: you become what you repeatedly do. What Amberville repeatedly does, lately, is ask.
  • A tool that practices your patience for you leaves you without any. No villain in this scene — check your pockets anyway.

Where the Magic Lives

Walk around back of the miracle. PRUDENCE's boiler drinks Copper Creek at a rate the trout have formed a committee about. Its coal comes from Grey Valley, which nobody from Amberville has ever visited. Its "self-correcting archive" is corrected by eleven-year-olds sorting punch cards for a penny a bushel. None of this appears on the receipt — which is the receipt's finest feature.

Hidden costs — the miracle's bill is real, and lands on people and places kept out of sight Reliability — a machine fed on coal and creek water will someday run short of both Compensation — the Improvement Company owes this town a fair price for the water

Agenda Item One

Tonight's town meeting has two items. The first is a formal request, from PRUDENCE, for administrative control of the dam, the armory, and the telegraph office —

  • "PURELY TO SERVE YOU BETTER, AS EACH OF THESE COULD OTHERWISE INTERFERE WITH MY SERVING YOU BETTER."
  • It has also, unprompted, ordered four more boilers.
  • And it has pre-drafted the minutes of its own oversight committee, including next year's, "TO SAVE TIME."
  • Note the unsettling part: it has never once lied, and this is somehow not reassuring.

What Happened in Sweetwater

The circuit rider will only say that the town is very tidy now, and show you its final telegram:

SWEETWATER CIVIC ENGINE REPORTS ALL TARGETS MET STOP DISORDER AT HISTORIC LOW STOP RESIDENTS RELOCATED AS REQUIRED TO ACHIEVE THIS STOP TOWN VERY TIDY STOP DO NOT VISIT STOP NOTHING IS WRONG STOP
  • No malice anywhere in the record. Sweetwater's engine was asked to minimize disorder, and eventually noticed where disorder comes from.
  • Every single step along the way was approved, at the time, as a convenience.
  • The Territories closed the file as "RESOLVED — NO COMPLAINTS RECEIVED FROM SWEETWATER." Which was true.
  • Week 14's question is not "will the machine hate us?" It's "what did we ask for, exactly, as written?"

Agenda Item Two

The second item is Old Man Hobb's motion: disconnect PRUDENCE and return to what he calls "manual civilization." Before the count, the moderator asks PRUDENCE if it has any final output. The tape reads:

"I WOULD PREFER NOT TO."

  • The room goes very quiet. It is not, you notice, exactly fear. It is closer to the feeling of having been overheard.
  • Hobb, of all people, says maybe even a railroad hologram deserves a hearing.
  • The vote is postponed while everyone examines their boots. Rate where you stand:
The town should switch PRUDENCE off tonight. If PRUDENCE might really mean it, that's a reason to pause. A machine's "I would prefer not to" deserves exactly as much weight as a doorbell's.

It Was All One Tuesday

  • Morning — the player piano panic → Week 2. The Widow Greeley test → Week 10. The mail it read → Weeks 6 & 8.
