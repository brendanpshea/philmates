What is PhilMates?

Most intros to philosophy ask students to read first and care later. PhilMates flips that: every lesson is a small narrative — Van Helsing's clinic, a courtroom of philosophers — that earns attention first, then teaches the concept underneath. They're built for a single class period or a solo study session.

Story first

Each topic is wrapped in a memorable scenario with characters and stakes, not a wall of definitions. Concepts arrive when you already want the answer.

Actually interactive

Multiple choice, fill-in-the-blank, and "where do you stand?" belief probes that revisit your earlier answers — so students see their own thinking change.

Works anywhere

Pure HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. No accounts, no tracking, no install. Progress saves locally, and completion reports cleanly to an LMS via SCORM.

Ai Ethics

High Noon in Amberville (Computing & AI Ethics — Overview)

One Tuesday in a frontier town that just installed a helpful brass civic engine — a comic sci-fi Western tour of computing and AI ethics: speech, ownership, money, surveillance, work, art, hidden costs, takeover, and one machine that would prefer not to be switched off.

⏱ ~20 min ❓ 9 questions

The Breadcrumb Network (Information Technology & Moral Values)

Hansel and Gretel invent writing, printing, telegraphy, and mass media to solve crises in a fairy-tale village — and unleash 2,500 years of unpredictable moral, cognitive, and political rearrangements.

⏱ ~15 min ❓ 5 questions

The Philosopher's Blueprint (Thinking, Writing & Timed Philosophy Essays)

PlatoBot's guide to the examined essay: why philosophy is worldview debugging, why writing builds cognitive agency in the age of AI, and how cognitive science helps you conquer the 1-hour timed bluebook exam.

⏱ ~15 min ❓ 4 questions

Bioethics

The Jekyll Protocol (Clinical Equipoise)

Dr. Helen Jekyll, great-granddaughter of the original, runs a modern clinical trial the right way — teaching the apparatus of research and the principle of equipoise against her ancestor's reckless self-experiment.

⏱ ~20 min ❓ 7 questions

Rounds with Van Helsing (Bioethics: Four Principles)

Train under Doctor Van Helsing in Transylvania and learn principlism — the four principles of bioethics — through the dilemmas of monstrous patients.

⏱ ~20 min ❓ 7 questions

House Calls in the Hedgerow (Patient Autonomy)

A country doctor in the world of Peter Rabbit learns what makes consent truly informed, and Emanuel's four models of the doctor–patient relationship.

⏱ ~20 min ❓ 6 questions

The Switch and the Scalpel (Trolley Problems & Medical Triage)

Explore what makes a moral dilemma through Philippa Foot and Judith Jarvis Thomson's classic trolley problems, Thomson's organ transplant puzzle, and real-world medical triage protocols.

⏱ ~20 min ❓ 10 questions

Ethical Theory

The Emerald Constitution (Mill's Harm Principle & Liberalism)

Princess Ozma's council revises the Wizard's old constitution — a mess of paternalism and negligence — using Mill's Harm Principle, with political and bioethical applications.

⏱ ~20 min ❓ 8 questions

The Cat at the Door (Kantian Deontology)

A cat asks a mouse where its friends are hiding. A story-driven tour of Kant's categorical imperative, who counts as an end, and how Korsgaard and O'Neill respond.

⏱ ~15 min ❓ 7 questions

The Ambassador's Manifest (Moral Status & the Moral Circle)

As the Moral Status Officer on a first-contact survey ship, you classify seven impossible aliens against Mary Anne Warren's checklist — and discover you have been ruling on animals, abortion, end-of-life care, AI, and extinction the whole time.

⏱ ~25 min ❓ 9 questions

The Midnight Tribunal (Utilitarianism)

You ate a $40 cake alone and now three philosophers haunt your dreams. A story-driven tour of hedonism, classical utilitarianism, and Peter Singer.

⏱ ~15 min ❓ 6 questions

The Labors of Arête (Virtue Ethics)

Athena guides you through Aristotle's virtue ethics — eudaimonia, the doctrine of the mean, phronesis, and the polis — using Greek myths as case studies.

⏱ ~20 min ❓ 8 questions

For Instructors

PhilMates is free and openly licensed (CC BY-NC 4.0) — use and adapt it for your classes. Three ways to run it — pick whatever fits your room and tools.

1

Project it

Open a lesson on the classroom display and click through together. The ↺ Reset button clears progress so you can re-run it live each period.

2

Share the link

Send students straight to a lesson URL for homework or flipped prep. Works on phones; progress saves on their device. Nothing to install.

3

Load into your LMS

Download the SCORM package below and upload it to Canvas, Blackboard, Moodle, or D2L. Completion and a score report to your gradebook automatically.

About

PhilMates grew out of a simple classroom observation: students engage with philosophy far more readily when an idea is a story they're inside than when it's a chapter they're assigned. Each lesson is designed as a short, self-contained activity that hooks first and explains second — and that a busy instructor can adopt without changing how they already run their course.

PhilMates is built and maintained by Brendan Shea, Professor of Philosophy and Computer Science at Rochester Community and Technical College and a Resident Fellow at the Minnesota Center for Philosophy of Science (University of Minnesota–Twin Cities). His teaching and research center on philosophy of science and applied ethics — especially bioethics and the ethics of AI — alongside logic and philosophical pedagogy. He teaches everything from Bioethics and Logic to Intro to Computer Science, and does a fair amount of public philosophy through popular writing, community courses, and service on scientific review boards.
