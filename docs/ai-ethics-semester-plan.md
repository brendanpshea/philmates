syllabus. New lessons live under `lessons/ai-ethics/`. Three existing
`ethical-theory` lessons slot in directly (weeks 3, 4, 15). Style notes:
narrative frame + relic-card thinkers + one signature widget per lesson (see
`AUTHORING.md`); slide-level structure of built lessons goes in

## Week 1 — High Noon in Amberville

Broad-overview opener (~30 min, not tied to any one lecture): one day in a
sci-fi Western town, played for Pratchett/Adams-style comedy. The frontier
town of Amberville takes delivery of **PRUDENCE** (Predictive Rationing,
Utility, Dispute-Elimination & Neighborliness Engine), a wardrobe-sized brass
difference engine, and the student — the town's *Municipal Analog Redundancy
Officer* — follows one Tuesday from dawn to the dusk town meeting. Each scene
raises one course theme: the notice board (speech), the saloon ballad (IP and
art), Glimmer ledger-scrip (digital cash), the sheriff's Thursday-crime list
(surveillance), the blacksmith and what the town stopped practicing (work,
virtues), the boiler's supply chain (environment, ghost work), the dam
request and "I would prefer not to" (x-risk, moral status), the Lucky
Prospector machine (games). Graded spine: recurring **Name That Worry**
questions that make students identify the ethical issue in each scene —
the course vocabulary used six-plus times without being lectured. Ungraded
"what should the town do?" belief checkpoints throughout; the dawn belief
probe pays off at dusk (and again in week 17). Replaces the earlier Oracle's
Ledger idea, whose semester-long probe is folded in. Built at
`lessons/ai-ethics/amberville/`; slide-by-slide in `phil_mates_outlines.md`.

## Week 2 — The Breadcrumb Network

Grimm's fairy-tale frame around the history of information ethics: Hansel & Gretel
in the village of Oakhaven introduce writing (bark runes), the printing press, the
telegraph, and mass media / the web, each time solving a village crisis and unleashing
an unintended moral rearrangement.
Thinkers: Plato (*Phaedrus*, King Thamus & memory), Erasmus vs. Luther (humanist
ideals vs. the pamphlet war), McLuhan & Postman ("the medium is the message" &
information-action ratio), and Melvin Kranzberg (Laws of Technology).
**Widget:** interactive information shock cascade engine.
Built at `lessons/ai-ethics/breadcrumb-network/`.

## Week 3 — Technology and Virtues

Reuse **The Labors of Arête** (`lessons/ethical-theory/virtue-ethics/`).
Optional tech-specific companion, **The Cursed Amulet**: a magical device that
whispers exactly what you want to hear, training your habits while worn.
**Widget:** a "habit forge" — daily phone practices raise/drain RPG-style
virtue stats (Temperance, Courage, Attention), with a golden-mean slider
between vices of excess and deficiency.

## Week 4 — Free Speech Online

Reuse **The Emerald Constitution** (`lessons/ethical-theory/harm-principle/`)
— Mill's harm principle with the clause-sorter. Optional sequel,
**The Moderator's Gauntlet**: night-shift moderator of an enchanted tavern's
notice board. **Widget:** a moderation queue (keep/remove/label posts); the
lesson then reveals which principle each call implicitly applied (harm,
offense, heckler's veto, misinformation) — and where the student was
inconsistent.

## Week 5 — The Philosopher's Blueprint (Essay 1)

Timed bluebook philosophy-essay bootcamp guided by PlatoBot (a malfunctioning Socrates-quoting automaton):
what philosophy is (worldview debugging), why writing develops non-transferable cognitive agency in the AI era,
and how Cognitive Load Theory supports the 10-minute outline scaffold (Thesis → Mechanism → Steelman → Rebuttal).
**Widget:** Essay Blueprint Builder with thesis tester, step-by-step mechanism, steelman vs strawman sorter, and 60-minute time budget. Reusable in any course with a writing assignment.

## Week 6 — The Songsmith's Ghost (Owning Ideas)

A bard's melody is copied note-for-note by a rival troupe's mimic-bird. Three
judges who disagree = the three IP theories: Locke's labor-mixing, utilitarian
incentives, Hegelian personality. **Widget:** case-sorter (sampling, fan
fiction, generic drugs, AI training data as a teaser) — assign each case to
the theory that best condemns or excuses it.

## Week 7 — The Dragon's Ledger (Digital Cash)

A village whose mint is guarded by a dragon (central banking, trust in one
keeper) vs. a rebel ledger copied into every villager's almanac.
**Widget:** toy blockchain — tamper with one block, watch every later hash
shatter. Ethics payload: speculation and scams, banking the unbanked,
ransomware, whether "trustlessness" is a virtue.

## Week 8 — The Thousand-Eyed Garden (Surveillance & Privacy)

Bentham returns (recurring character from the utilitarianism lesson) as
architect of the Panopticon tower at the garden's center.
**Widget:** data-trail visualizer — pick five mundane actions from your day,
watch inferences accumulate (the aggregation effect) until the garden knows
you're pregnant/broke/leaving. Concepts: chilling effects, "nothing to hide,"
Nissenbaum's contextual integrity.

## Week 9 — The Gauntlet of Six Gates (Exam 1 review)

Boss-rush: one gate per lecture 1–6, each guarded by that unit's key thinker
demanding a mini-quiz. Retrieval practice dressed as a dungeon; the built-in
↺ Reset makes it re-runnable live in class.

## Week 10 — The Masquerade of Silverport (What Is AI?)

Imitation-game masked ball — unmask the automaton among the guests — with
Searle's Chinese Room as a literal locked room mid-lesson: *you* push symbols
you don't understand, then the lesson asks whether the room understood the
conversation you just aced. **Widget:** judge chat transcripts as human or
machine, then get the reveal and your accuracy score.

## Week 11 — The Loom and the Lark (Work and Art)

Two threads: the historical Luddites (smashed looms over labor conditions,
not technology-hatred — misconception worth its own MCQ) and a clockwork lark
that outsings the town bard. **Widget:** "who made this?" gallery — guess
human vs. machine for poems and pictures — then the harder question of
whether inability to tell settles anything about art.

## Week 12 — The Hungry Golem (Environmental & Social Impact)

The kingdom's miracle golem drinks a river a day; its clay is dug by distant
villagers nobody in the capital has met. Data-center water/energy, mineral
extraction, ghost-work data labeling in one figure. **Widget:** trace the
golem's supply chain on a map, uncovering a hidden cost at each node. Echoes
the Mechanical Turk's hidden operator (week 2).

## Week 13 — The Advocate's Mirror (Essay 2)

Steelmanning sequel to week 5: a mirror that argues back; you pass only by
making your opponent's case as strong as possible before answering it.
**Widget:** rank candidate objections from strawman to steelman, then match
each strong objection to its best reply.

## Week 14 — The Wish Engine (End of the World)

A found genie-machine that grants wishes with perfect literalism — King Midas
as proto-alignment-failure, a paperclip-style cursed artifact, orthogonality
and instrumental convergence as "rules of the engine."
**Signature widget:** phrase a wish, see the catastrophic literal reading,
iterate over three rounds — specification gaming made visceral.

## Week 15 — Robot Rights / Moral Status

Reuse **The Ambassador's Manifest** (`lessons/ethical-theory/moral-status/`) —
this is its home fixture. Optional AI-specific sequel, **The Tin Woodman's
Trial**: courtroom hearing on whether a woodman who *performs* pain can be
wronged. Sentience vs. sapience, moral patients vs. agents, precautionary
"what if we're wrong?"

## Week 16 — The Arcade at the End of the World (Games and Ethics)

Suits' lusory attitude and the magic circle (why cheating ruins the game even
when nobody's harmed), the amoralist challenge about virtual violence,
predatory design. **Widget:** loot-box simulator with honest odds displayed —
feel the pull, then see the math and the dark patterns named.

## Week 17 — The Gauntlet, Part II (Exam 2 review)

Boss-rush over lectures 7–12, ending with the `<phil-beliefs-review>` payoff
of week 1's Oracle's Ledger.

## Build priorities

- **Soonest needed:** The Mechanical Turk (week 2, semester starts Aug 24).
- **Best bang-for-effort:** The Wish Engine (14), The Thousand-Eyed Garden
  (8), The Masquerade of Silverport (10) — each has a signature interactive
  that carries the whole concept.
- **Most reusable across courses:** the two essay-week lessons (5, 13).
- **Connected-universe touches:** Bentham reappears as Panopticon architect
  (8); the Mechanical Turk's hidden operator echoes in the Hungry Golem's
  ghost workers (12).
